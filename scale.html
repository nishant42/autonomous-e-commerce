    Phase 1 · Test (months 1–4)

    Goal: learn which frames sell before committing money to stock. 24 candidate models go live as pre-orders with honest ship dates; a bandit moves ad spend to the ones that convert; only winners get small air-freighted batches. In month 1 technology costs against of sales (). With your developer inputs the all-in figure is ( of sales), which falls quickly because almost all of it is fixed.

    Figure S.1: One region, one small database, apps that cost nothing when idle. The Action Gateway is a module, but it already enforces limits, idempotency and the audit log.

    What runs, and why it is enough

    • Container Apps consumption plan: one always-on gateway replica (billed at the idle rate most of the time), agents and jobs that scale from zero. The monthly free grant covers most of it.
    • Postgres Flexible Server B1ms: ~10–85 orders a day is a tiny load. No HA yet (Burstable doesn't offer it); point-in-time restore covers mistakes. If Postgres is down, Shopify keeps selling.
    • Service Bus Standard, Key Vault, App Configuration free tier, Log Analytics within its free 5 GB.
    • Staging + PR previews: scale-to-zero apps and a second B1ms with seeded data.
    • Shopify plan is chosen automatically: the cheapest plan once card fees are counted.

    Agents, limits, gate

    • 11 agents: Product Test, Creative + Brand Guardian, Budget Allocator (bandit), Order Orchestrator, Procurement (small), Supplier Liaison (email), Compliance, Care Triage + Resolution, Reconciliation, Sentinel.
    • Limits: a £1k PO, a £30 refund, a ±5% price move and a capped daily ad budget per model go through on their own. Anything bigger asks an owner.
    • Exit gate: 3+ models above 2% conversion with positive first-order margin after acquisition cost; 50+ orders/day for 14 days; one factory delivered two batches on time.

    Phase 2 · Traction (months 5–9)

    Goal: make growth reliable. The database gets a high-availability standby, the gateway runs two replicas, Redis takes the spend counters, and Front Door fronts our APIs and owner console.

    Figure S.2: The same code, split along existing module boundaries. The gateway is the only identity that can reach payment and supplier credentials.

    What changes

    • Postgres B1ms → D2ds_v5 with a zone-redundant HA standby (billed as a second server).
    • Azure Managed Redis B1 for exact spend limits under concurrency.
    • Front Door Standard with custom WAF rules in front of webhooks, the owner console and the supplier portal.
    • App Configuration Standard for feature flags and kill switches with change history.

    Agents, limits, gate

    • +8 agents (19): Forecasting, Replenishment Planner, Inventory Position, Pricing + Price Guard, Promotion Planner, Lifecycle, Returns, Accounts Payable, plus the Buy Panel.
    • Limits: PO £4k, refund £60, price ±10%.
    • Exit gate: 500 orders/day; database p95 CPU above 60% at peaks; non-UK cards above 15% of orders.

    Phase 3 · Scale (months 10–16)

    Goal: industrial reliability. Workloads move to dedicated nodes spread across availability zones, the ledger gets a disaster-recovery replica in a second UK region, and container security scanning switches on.

    Figure S.3: Larger tiers of the same services. Private networking, API Management and a lakehouse are optional add-ons you can switch on in the inputs.

    What changes

    • Container Apps dedicated plan: 3 × D4 nodes (4 vCPU, 16 GiB) across zones, plus the plan management fee.
    • Postgres D4ds_v5 with HA, plus a DR replica in UK West.
    • Defender for Containers on all vCores; Container Registry Standard; Xero Comprehensive for multi-currency.
    • Nightly LightGBM + reconciliation job replaces statsforecast once the backtest says it wins.

    Agents, limits, gate

    • +10 agents (29): Demand Sensing, Node Allocation, Inbound Coordinator, Delivery Exception, Supplier Scorecard, Competitor Monitor, Growth Strategist, Cash & Budget, Auditor, Incident Commander.
    • Limits: PO £15k on its own, up to £45k with the panel; refund £120; price ±15%.
    • Exit gate: 2,000 orders/day; a 10× peak load test passes; human touches under 10 per 1,000.

    Phase 4 · Million (months 17–24)

    Goal: one million customers across the UK, EU and US. By default this stays lean: three dedicated servers across availability zones and a 4-vCPU database with a standby and a disaster-recovery copy in UK West, because the load is under one order per second. A second region (US East) with its own runtime cell is an option in "Your inputs"; it adds resilience against a whole region failing, not speed.

    Figure S.4: The optional two-region layout. The default is one region with a DR database copy. Either way there is one write primary: at under one order per second that is enough, and nothing a shopper waits on runs here.

    What changes

    • Default: 3 dedicated D4 servers across zones and Postgres D4ds_v5 with HA standby + UK West DR copy (512 GB). Option: 2 regions, more servers, D8 database, more storage. Each is a switch in "Your inputs".
    • Front Door Premium for managed WAF rules and bot protection; Container Registry Premium with geo-replication.
    • Optional: Event Hubs for clickstream, Fabric for analytics, private networking for a stricter security posture.

    Agents, limits

    • +4 agents (33): Market Localisation, Marketplace, Wholesale (EDI), multi-entity Close & Reporting.
    • Limits: PO £40k on its own, up to £120k with the panel. Refunds stay at £120: refund risk grows with fraud, not with volume.

    Why Shopify still wins

    • Building our own removes the Shopify plan (up to £1,800/month) but Stripe's published card rates are higher than Shopify Plus rates, and fraud screening (£0.04 per payment) and US sales tax (0.5%) are extra.
    • It also needs extra developers to build and run checkout, accounts, product pages, tax and fraud handling.
    • So at scale the "own storefront" scenario costs more, not less. It only makes sense for a shopping experience Shopify can't support.

    Why SES from phase 3

    • Shopify Email is free up to 10,000 emails and needs no engineering, so it's the right start.
    • At millions of emails a month, Amazon SES at $0.10 per 1,000 is about a tenth of the price.
    • The switch costs one or two weeks to build templates, unsubscribe and bounce handling. Email sits behind an adapter, so agents don't change.

    The product-testing engine (phase 1)

    A · Test demand

    24 models are photographed from samples. Each gets a few ad creatives and a product page with honest pre-order dates. The Budget Allocator runs Thompson sampling: every model starts with a small, equal budget and spend shifts automatically to the ones that convert.

    B · Decide by rule

    After at least 1,000 sessions per model: scale if P(conversion > 2%) > 90% and first-order margin after acquisition cost is positive; kill if it falls below 10% or the spend cap is hit. The rule lives in the gateway's policy, not in a prompt.

    C · Buy small, then bigger

    Winners get 150–200-unit batches by air to fulfil pre-orders. Proven sellers move to sea freight at volume prices. Losers are never stocked.

    Trade-offs

    Trade-off 1

    Shopify Inbox + our own agent vs a help-desk product

    Chosen
    Shopify Inbox (included) and a shared mailbox, answered by the Resolution agent; owners see escalations in our owner console.
    Gave up
    A polished help-desk UI for humans.
    Why
    Help desks bill per ticket, including tickets our agent answers. The agent already keeps conversation memory in our ledger. Gorgias stays one switch away as an add-on.
    Trade-off 2

    Temporal from day one

    Chosen
    Temporal Cloud pay-as-you-go from month 1; it costs pennies at test volume.
    Why
    Rewriting purchase-order sagas mid-growth is the most expensive migration on the list.
    Trade-off 3

    One write region vs multi-master

    Chosen
    One Postgres primary with a promotable geo-replica.
    Why
    At under one order per second the platform isn't latency-critical, and multi-master conflicts on money and stock are a correctness risk.
    Trade-off 4

    Public endpoints with strong identity vs private networking

    Chosen
    Managed identities, TLS and Front Door; private networking is an optional add-on.
    Gave up
    Network-level isolation, which some auditors prefer.
    Why
    Card data never touches our platform (Shopify keeps PCI scope). Service Bus Premium alone would cost more than the rest of the phase 3 data tier.
