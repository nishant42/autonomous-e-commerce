Deep dive D · Supplier interaction

No perfect API: the supplier integration ladder

Suppliers talk however they talk. The platform absorbs that variety with one canonical supplier model and a ladder of channel adapters, from structured and reliable at the top to flexible and fragile at the bottom. Agents reason about POs, acknowledgements and ASNs, never about transports.

Supplier capability profile

Each supplier has a declarative profile. The Supplier Liaison chooses channels from it, and the fallback order is explicit.

supplier: sup_yd            # Yves Delorme (hypothetical relationship)
contacts:
  account_manager: { email_domain: "verified-supplier-domain.example", verified_phone_ref: "kv://..." }
channels:
  purchase_order:   [edi_850, portal, email_pdf]      # try in order
  po_ack:           [edi_855, email]
  asn:              [edi_856, email_packing_list]
  invoice:          [edi_810, email_pdf]
  price_list:       [email_xlsx]                        # quarterly
  stock_availability: [portal_readonly]                 # checked before expedite requests
  expedite_request: [email]                             # human-to-human style, templated + LLM paragraph
sla:
  po_ack_hours: 48
  chase_ladder: [ "email reminder @48h", "second reminder @72h", "account manager @96h", "T3 owner brief @120h" ]
terms:
  moq_eur: 1500
  payment: "30 days EOM"
  map_policy: true
  expedite_fee_max_pct: 8

Supplier onboarding is part of the design

Onboarding a new supplier means filling in a capability profile, building or configuring the channel adapters, running a parallel month (the Liaison drafts everything, and a verifier compares against what the supplier actually sends), and only then enabling T0/T1 actions for that supplier. It also includes a commercial push: moving the supplier up the ladder. That is often negotiable once order volume is meaningful.
