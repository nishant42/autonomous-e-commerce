10 · Key trade-offs

The decisions that could have gone the other way

Each decision records what was chosen, what was given up, and the signal that would make me revisit it.

Trade-off 1

Many narrow agents vs one general "operator" agent

Chosen
About 30 narrow agents with small tool sets, coordinated by events and workflows.
Gave up
Simplicity of one agent and cross-domain reasoning "for free".
Why
Reliability falls as tool count and context grow. Narrow agents can be evaluated, permissioned and quarantined separately. Cross-domain reasoning is handled explicitly through case records and panels.
Revisit if
Coordination overhead (hand-off latency, duplicated context) becomes the dominant failure mode.
Trade-off 2

LLM proposes, deterministic gateway executes

Chosen
No direct side effects from models. Typed proposals only.
Gave up
Some agility: every new action type needs a schema, a policy and an adapter.
Why
It makes safety provable and audit complete, and it keeps credentials out of prompts.
Revisit if
Never for money or stock. Possibly relax for read-only exploratory tools.
Trade-off 3

Choreography + orchestration hybrid vs pure event choreography

Chosen
Events between domains, Temporal workflows inside them.
Gave up
One uniform model; the team must understand both.
Why
With pure choreography, "where is PO 1234 stuck?" is hard to answer and compensation is hard to guarantee. With pure orchestration, domains become coupled.
Revisit if
Workflow count explodes with little logic in each. Then fold simple ones back into event handlers.
Trade-off 4

Own Ops Ledger vs a full ERP as system of record

Chosen
Our own event-driven Ops Ledger, plus accounting SaaS for statutory books.
Gave up
Out-of-the-box ERP features and an established audit story.
Why
ERPs are built for human clerks and batch processes. Agents need low-latency, event-native state with proposal and decision history.
Revisit if
Multi-entity or multi-country complexity grows. Then adopt Business Central or NetSuite behind the same Ledger events.
Trade-off 5

Autonomy thresholds: speed vs safety

Chosen
Conservative T0 limits at launch, widened by evidence (Auditor precision above 99% for 30 days lifts a limit by one step).
Gave up
Some early margin and speed from verification latency.
Why
Trust is earned per action type with data, the way a new employee's signing authority grows.
Revisit
Continuously, driven by metrics rather than opinion.
Trade-off 6

Verifier agents: cost and latency vs error rate

Chosen
Verifiers on T2, on all money-moving extractions and on customer-facing text. Sampling for T0/T1.
Gave up
Roughly 20-35% more LLM spend and a few seconds of latency on verified paths.
Why
One wrong supplier price accepted, or one false promise to a customer, costs more than a month of verifier tokens.
Revisit if
A verifier's catch rate drops close to zero over a large sample. Then downgrade it to sampling.
Trade-off 7

Browser automation for supplier portals

Chosen
Allowed as the second-to-last rung, behind deterministic scripts, with an LLM only for self-healing selectors. Read-mostly.
Gave up
Robustness: portals change without notice, and there are terms-of-service considerations.
Why
Some suppliers offer nothing else, and "no perfect API" is a stated constraint.
Revisit
At each supplier review. Push for EDI or API in the contract when volume justifies it.
Trade-off 8

Single cloud (Azure) vs best-of-breed multi-cloud

Chosen
Azure plus SaaS, with open interfaces (OTel, CloudEvents, an OpenAI-compatible gateway, Postgres).
Gave up
Some best-of-breed options and some negotiating leverage.
Why
One identity plane (Entra) for agents and services, private networking and a single compliance boundary. For a small platform team, operational simplicity wins.
Revisit if
A required model or capability is unavailable in-region.
Trade-off 9

Scarcity pricing on premium goods

Chosen
Allowed within narrow bands (≤ +10%), honouring MAP and parity rules, and never on essentials during complaints.
Gave up
Some short-term margin optimisation.
Why
Brand trust and the supplier relationship (a premium supplier's price policy) matter more than squeezing out a spike.
Revisit
With supplier agreement and customer-sentiment data.
Trade-off 10

Thin in-house harness vs a full agent framework

Chosen
A thin harness we own, on Foundry model hosting and tracing, plus MCP tools.
Gave up
Framework conveniences such as graph builders and prebuilt memory.
Why
The parts that decide safety (the tool loop, validation boundary, memory hygiene and proposal semantics) are exactly what frameworks abstract away. We keep them explicit and tested.
Revisit if
A framework offers these as explicit, testable primitives. Adopting one would be low-risk because agents already talk through standard protocols.
