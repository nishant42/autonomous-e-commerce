Agents at a glance

Ten departments, one line per agent

Nine business departments plus a Control Tower. Each department has a thin supervisor that only routes work, and specialist agents with one job each. Money and maths are handled by plain code and ML; language models are used for reading, writing and judgement.

★ key agent LLM ML Rules Workflow Code

01Commerce

Customer orders

  • Order Orchestrator: takes each customer order through payment, fraud check, warehouse and shipping. Workflow Code
  • Fraud Screening: checks whether an order is fake; the LLM only reviews the unclear cases. ML LLM
  • Catalogue Agent: turns supplier product files into clean product pages; a checker approves them. LLM

02Demand & Inventory

What will sell, and what we have

  • Forecasting Agent: predicts how many of each product will sell in the coming weeks, as a range. ML
  • Demand Sensing: spots sudden changes, such as a towel selling 3× faster. ML
  • Inventory Position: counts stock on the shelf, reserved and on the way. Code
  • Replenishment Planner: calculates how many to buy and when, e.g. "buy 240". Optimiser
  • Planner Explainer: writes in plain English why the planner chose that number. LLM

03Procurement

Buying stock

  • Procurement Agent: turns the planner's number into a purchase order, checks cash and supplier terms, and sends it to the Action Gateway. LLM Calculators Inside: PO Builder builds the order · Budget Checker confirms we can afford it · Buy Panel: three agents vote on large or unusual orders.

04Supplier Management

Talking to Yves Delorme and others

  • Supplier Liaison: talks to suppliers by API, EDI, file, email or portal; sends POs and reads replies. LLM RPA Inside: Document Extractor reads emails and PDFs · Verifier double-checks what was read.
  • Supplier Scorecard: rates each supplier on on-time delivery and correct quantities. Code Stats

05Logistics

Coordinating the outsourced 3PL

  • Inbound Coordinator: tracks supplier deliveries coming into the warehouse. Workflow
  • Node Allocation: decides which warehouse (UK or EU) ships each order. Optimiser
  • Delivery Exception: handles lost, late or damaged parcels; reships up to €250. LLM Rules

06Pricing

Prices and promotions

  • Pricing Agent: sets prices, never below the profit floor. ML Rules Inside: Price Guard blocks any bad price, e.g. €9 for a €79 towel.
  • Promotion Planner: plans discounts and sales. ML
  • Competitor Monitor: watches competitor prices. Code

07Marketing

Demand creation, stock-aware

  • Growth Strategist: plans campaigns and announces them in advance so the forecast can account for them. LLM
  • Budget Allocator: moves ad money towards products that sell well and have stock. Bandit
  • Creative Agent: writes ad copy and emails; Brand Guardian checks tone and claims. LLM
  • Lifecycle / CRM Agent: sends "back in stock" and abandoned-basket emails. LLM Rules

08Customer Care

Questions, complaints and refunds

  • Triage: reads each customer message and sorts it (where is my order, refund, complaint). Small LLM
  • Resolution Agent: answers using real order data. LLM Rules Inside: Refund Policy rules decide the refund; the LLM only writes the reply · Reply QA checks it before sending.
  • Voice of Customer: finds common complaints, e.g. "many say the towel shrinks". LLM

09Finance

Mostly plain code: no model guesses with money

  • Reconciliation: checks every hour that our numbers match Stripe and the warehouse. Code
  • Accounts Payable: pays a supplier only if order, delivery and invoice all match. Code
  • Cash & Budget: tracks cash and sets how much each department may spend. Code
  • Close & Reporting: produces the monthly accounts. Code

10Control Tower

Safety only: no business work

  • Sentinel: watches all agents and automatically quarantines one that behaves strangely. ML
  • Incident Commander: handles emergencies and coordinates the fix. LLM
  • Auditor: re-checks a sample of yesterday's decisions; too many mistakes reduces that agent's autonomy. LLM Rules

The story: a viral post makes a towel sell 3× faster

An interiors influencer posts about the Yves Delorme Étoile towel. Sales jump from 3 to 9 a day. This is how every department reacts, with no human involved.

  1. Commerce
    Orders flow in. Customers buy on Shopify and pay with Stripe; the Order Orchestrator sends each order to the warehouse. Plain software, no AI needed.
  2. Demand & Inventory
    Spot it. Demand Sensing notices the towel selling 3× faster than forecast and posts an alert on the event bus.
  3. Commerce
    Is it real? Fraud Screening confirms 311 distinct real customers, not bots or a fraud ring.
  4. Demand & Inventory
    Re-forecast. The Forecasting Agent predicts about 9 a day, fading back over 3 weeks. Inventory Position: only 42 left, under 3 days of cover.
  5. Marketing · Pricing
    Don't make it worse. The Budget Allocator pauses ads on the towel and moves budget to well-stocked products. The Pricing Agent ends the 10% discount, but does not raise the price, to protect the premium brand.
  6. Procurement
    Buy more. The Replenishment Planner simulates thousands of futures to size the order. The Procurement Agent writes the PO and checks cash. Because demand is unusual, the Action Gateway raises the risk tier and the Buy Panel must agree: 180 now, review in 10 days.
  7. Supplier Mgmt
    Talk to the supplier. The Supplier Liaison sends the PO to Yves Delorme by EDI and emails an expedite request. Reply: "120 in 5 days, 60 in 3 weeks, €380 fee." One agent extracts it, a second verifies it, and the PO is amended.
  8. Customer Care
    Look after customers. When stock runs out the site shows an honest pre-order date, affected customers are emailed proactively, and new visitors can join a "notify me" list.
  9. Logistics
    Stock arrives. On day 7, 120 towels arrive. The Inbound Coordinator confirms receipt, backorders ship first, and the Lifecycle agent emails the waitlist. Ads resume gradually.
  10. Finance
    Pay the supplier. Accounts Payable pays only when order, delivery and invoice match. Reconciliation checks our numbers against Stripe and the warehouse.
  11. Control Tower
    Safety throughout. The Sentinel watches every agent and would quarantine one instantly if it misbehaved, or halt purchasing if the spike turned out to be fraud.
Human touches: zero

An owner would only be paged for a PO above €60k, a split panel verdict, a supplier changing bank details, or confirmed fraud. No agent moved money directly: every action passed through the Action Gateway's limits first.

Viral post → Demand Sensing → Fraud check → Re-forecast → Ads paused, discount ended → PO approved by panel → Sent to Yves Delorme → Customers informed → Stock arrives → Supplier paid · Control Tower watching throughout

How they work together

Agents never act directly and never chat in free text. They announce facts as events, long jobs such as a purchase order run in a workflow engine, and every real action (money, stock, prices, messages to suppliers) goes through one Action Gateway that checks limits and risk tier.
