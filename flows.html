06 · Procurement and marketing flows

The two loops that run the business

The supply loop turns demand into stock and cash movements. The demand loop keeps marketing spend matched to what can actually be sold. They meet at stock cover: the number of days of demand the available stock can serve.

Avoiding a hidden feedback loop

Marketing and pricing change the very demand that forecasting learns from. Google's "Hidden Technical Debt in ML Systems" paper calls this a hidden feedback loop: the model ends up learning from its own effects. Three rules stop it: every campaign and price change is published as a plan before it runs and becomes a forecast feature; about 5% of eligible SKU-days are held out from promotions to measure true lift; and every consumer of the forecast is registered, so a model change never surprises an undeclared user.

Why deterministic coupling rules?

The loop must be stable. If two LLM agents "negotiated" every hour, you would get oscillation and cost you cannot explain. The thresholds are policy, the responses inside each band are agent judgement, and hysteresis (e.g. leave "tight" only when cover goes above 5 weeks) prevents flapping.
