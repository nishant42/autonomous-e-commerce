Is it the product, or is it the site? The agent that decides what to kill

You said it yourself: of ten launches, two do well, two are ambiguous and need tweaking — and you do not know whether the problem is the product or the site — and the rest is crap you get rid of. That ambiguity is the most expensive thing in the whole model, so the third agent I would build is the one that resolves it. It is also the agent that tells the other two where to spend, which is why I chose it over pricing or customer care.

This is implemented, not just described

The engine in engine/ runs these rules on 90 days of seeded history with no dependencies: cd engine && python3 run.py ui opens the operator console, where the four verdicts below appear with their benchmark bands and the kill sits in the approval queue — which is the only place it can be signed off. This static snapshot shows the same run.

The short version
  • Few orders has four different causes — traffic, site, offer, economics — and each has a different remedy. The agent separates them by funnel signature, not by the order count.
  • A product that was out of stock is never killed. It was being judged on sales it was not allowed to make. This is the towel case arriving in a different agent.
  • Every product gets a fixed experiment budget. When it is spent without a verdict, the default is kill, not "let it run another month".
  • A kill is never autonomous. It stops spend, clears stock and releases a domain, so it is held for a human with the whole case attached. Everything else — fix the site, reprice, new creative, scale — the agent does.

Read the two ambiguous ones

The mug and the candle would both show up in a spreadsheet as "sells a bit, loses money". They get opposite verdicts, for reasons a person can check in one line each: the mug's buyers want it and the checkout loses them; the candle's visitors see it, decline it, and the ones who buy send it back. That is the distinction you said you cannot currently make, and it is worth more than any single model in the system.

Experiment budget: the mechanism that forces a decision

Each product is launched with a fixed budget to earn its verdict — €1,500 in the configuration — tracked as a hard counter in the gateway. Three consequences:

StateAgent behaviourWhy it matters
Budget remaining, exposure not metCONTINUE_TEST; the marketing agent may keep spending within the daily capsProtects young products from being killed by noise
Budget remaining, a defect foundFix the site or reprice, then re-judge after the change has its own exposureStops "we killed it but the checkout was broken"
Budget exhausted, no verdict reachedKILL by default, held for a humanThe expensive failure mode is indecision, not a wrong kill. A product that cannot prove itself for €1,500 does not get €4,000
Budget exhausted on a winnerProduct graduates: budget moves from the experiment pool to the operating poolMakes "scaling" an explicit, funded decision rather than a drift

The marketing agent enforces the same counter from the other side: once a product's experiment budget is spent, the gateway refuses any proposal that would raise its spend. Two agents, one number, enforced in one place.

The kill runbook

A kill is cheap to recommend and expensive to execute badly, so the agent produces the full sequence and a human signs it:

Immediately

Stop the bleeding

All acquisition spend on the product off in the same cycle (the marketing agent consumes verdict.published), retargeting and email flows off, experiment budget closed.

Same day

Stop the capital

Cancel open purchase orders not yet shipped; flag any in transit for the markdown plan rather than the shelf.

Days 1–60

Convert stock to cash

Three-step markdown curve sized to clear within 60 days, with the floor set by landed cost rather than by hope. The pricing rules already know the floor.

After clearance

Close it down

Storefront stays live while stock clears, then archived; the domain release is a separate human signal, because it is irreversible and outward-facing.

Permanent

Keep the lesson

The finding is written to portfolio memory as a prior for the next launch: category, price point, creative angle, refund reason, and which stage failed.

Harness and policy

agent: product_verdict
version: 3
model:
  plan: claude-sonnet-5-5
  max_steps: 40
  token_budget_month: 400_000

reads: [funnel_rates, ad_metrics_panel, margin_table, stock_cover,
        experiment_budget, returns_by_variant, portfolio_memory]

writes: [publish_verdict, raise_anomaly]

policy:
  min_sessions: 1_500             # exposure gate: below this there is no verdict
  min_spend_eur: 400
  payback_factor: 1.2
  experiment_budget_eur: 1_500
  window_days: 14
  kill_requires_human: true       # hard-coded T3 regardless of value

verify:
  - exposure_met_or_verdict_is_CONTINUE_TEST
  - no_kill_while_any_variant_out_of_stock
  - every_rate_cited_has_evidence_id
  - verdict_in_enum

The benchmark bands live in engines/funnel.py and are per category, which is the only part a merchandiser should ever need to tune. Everything else is the same code for every product and every vertical.

What a human gets each Monday

One page: ten products, each with a verdict, a confidence, the stage that failed, the ranked fixes, the remaining experiment budget, and — for the kills — the runbook with a single approval. The decisions a human must make shrink to the irreversible ones, which at ten products is a short meeting rather than a week of spreadsheet archaeology.
