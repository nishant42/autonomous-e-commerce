01 · Company / domain architecture

Departments as bounded contexts

The company is modelled the way a well-run retailer is organised. There are nine operating departments, each a bounded context with its own data, its own events and its own agents. There is also a thin governance layer where the owners set the envelope.

Key idea
  • Departments exist to create ownership boundaries, not to copy a human org chart. Each department is the single writer of its data and publishes events for everyone else.
  • One cross-cutting Control Tower (Risk & Exceptions) watches every other department. It can pause any of them, but it never runs their business logic.

The governance boundary: what humans still do

Humans do (outside daily operations)

  • Approve the envelope: monthly spend caps, margin floors, maximum discount, refund policy, brand voice.
  • Sign supplier contracts and grant legal authority (e.g. a PO signing mandate up to €X).
  • Handle T3 exceptions: novel situations, legal threats, safety incidents.
  • Review a weekly autonomous operations report and the evaluation dashboards.

Humans do not do

  • Approve routine POs, price changes, refunds or campaigns inside the envelope.
  • Answer customer emails or supplier emails.
  • Reconcile payouts, chase invoices or book journals.
  • Watch dashboards to keep things running. The Control Tower does this.
Why model it this way?

With bounded contexts, the blast radius stays small. A misbehaving pricing agent can be quarantined without affecting procurement or customer care. They also give each agent a small, testable mandate. A narrow agent with 6 tools is far more reliable than a "CEO agent" with 60.
