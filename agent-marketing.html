Women 25–45: how the targeting is built, and how the budget follows the evidence

The brief asked two things. How do we actually target a defined customer base with real tools, and — more importantly — how do we stay on top of it well enough to move budget towards what worked and away from what did not. The second question is the hard one, because platform-reported performance is systematically flattering, and most reallocation decisions in small e-commerce are made on noise.

The short version
  • Targeting is mostly not targeting any more. On Meta and Google the audience is found by the auction, not by picked interests. The controllable levers are creative, offer, geography, exclusions and signal quality. The agent spends its effort there.
  • The agent never optimises ROAS. It optimises contribution margin per order and new-customer CAC, computed from Shopify cost data, returns and payment fees — not from platform-reported revenue.
  • Four clocks, not one. Hourly guards, daily pause-and-scale, weekly channel reallocation, monthly incrementality. Each clock has its own statistical bar, and the fast clocks are allowed to do only cheap, reversible things.
  • No decision below minimum exposure. A cell with 11 conversions is not a loser; it is unmeasured. The single most common way money is wasted is by acting on cells that never had a chance to speak.
This is implemented, not just described

The engine in engine/ runs these rules on 90 days of seeded history with no dependencies. cd engine && python3 run.py ui opens an operator console at 127.0.0.1:8787 where a cycle can be run and watched job by job, proposals approved or rejected, and every number clicked back to the query that produced it. python3 run.py demo prints the same decisions in a terminal, and this static snapshot is what run.py console generates — the same decisions, read back out of the database, with every intermediate number, the gateway's verdict on each proposal and the full audit trail.

Verification, not assumption

The audience hypothesis is checked every week against who actually bought, by joining Shopify customers to self-reported survey answers and platform demographic breakdowns. If the buyers turn out to be women 35–55, that becomes a finding the verdict agent uses for the next product launch, and a creative brief for this one. The system is designed to discover the customer, not to defend the brief.

Signal quality: the lever almost nobody pulls

Before any clever allocation, the conversion signal the platforms learn from has to be correct and complete. A broken or lossy signal makes every downstream decision wrong, and it is the single most common defect in small stores.

The model layer: 35 features, and what earns its place

Rule bands answer "is this cell inside target". They do not answer "what is driving it, and what would happen if we changed something". That is a modelling problem, and it is run as one: engines/ml.py builds a panel of 35 features per cell-day and hands nothing to the allocator until it has beaten the naive baselines out of sample.

How the model reaches a decision, and how far it is allowed to
Decision
The allocator asks the promoted model for an expected CPA per cell, blends it with the observation, and the deterministic bands still decide. If the two disagree by more than a factor of two, the observation wins and the gap is logged as an anomaly.
Reason
A model that is wrong should cost a suboptimal budget split, never an unbounded one. The bands are the containment; the model is the improvement inside it.
Consequence
With no promoted model, or a stale one, the agent behaves exactly as it did before. Model availability is never a dependency for the business running.
On the numbers you will see in the console

The demo panel is generated from a known process, so the out-of-sample R² near 0.98 is a property of the generator, not a forecast of live performance. On a real store with six months of history, expect roughly 0.3 to 0.6 on weekly orders per cell. The honest measure is whether the pipeline beats the previous-window baseline, and the workflow and guards are what carry over to real data.

The four clocks

Different decisions deserve different speeds and different statistical bars. Running everything on one daily loop is how budgets end up chasing noise.

Weekly rule (between channels)

Channel-level allocation is a bandit problem with a slow clock: each channel is an arm, the reward is CM2 per euro spent, and the agent samples from the posterior rather than greedily chasing last week's winner.

weekly_allocation:
  arms: [meta_prospecting, meta_retargeting, google_search, google_pmax, tiktok, klaviyo_paid_push]
  reward: CM2_per_euro            # from our ledger, 14-day attribution window, returns net
  method: thompson_sampling       # Beta-Binomial on "profitable order per € band"
  priors: inherited from the two best-performing past products (cross-product memory)
  constraints:
    min_share_per_arm: 0.05       # never starve an arm to the point it stops being measurable
    max_share_per_arm: 0.55       # never a single point of failure
    max_weekly_change_per_arm: 0.40
    exploration_floor: 0.10       # 10% of budget always on untested creative or arms
    holdout_geo_share: 0.10       # measurement, not spend efficiency
  escalate_if:
    total_budget_change_pct > 25  # a big swing goes to a human with the evidence attached
    any_arm_to_zero: true
Why a bandit and not "move money to the best ROAS"
Decision
Sample allocations from the posterior distribution of each channel's profit per euro, with a 10% exploration floor and a 5% minimum share.
Reason
Greedy reallocation on small samples is a machine for amplifying noise: the arm that got lucky last week gets the money, stops being compared fairly, and the system locks onto it. With 50–300 orders a week per product, the noise is larger than most of the differences being acted on.
Consequence
Slightly lower short-term efficiency in exchange for not losing the genuinely better channel, and a posterior that becomes a reusable prior for the next product launch.

What the holdout row changes

Reported orders across the paid cells total 129. The holdout says roughly 9% of orders would have happened anyway. The agent therefore discounts incremental performance by that factor before the weekly reallocation, which moved the "fabric close-up" cell from "below target" to "clearly unprofitable" and protected the system from scaling retargeting, whose apparent €12 CPA is mostly harvesting. Without the holdout, the correct decision and the wrong decision look identical.

The harness for this agent

agent: marketing_allocator
version: 4
model:
  plan: claude-sonnet-5-5
  max_steps: 10
  token_budget_month: 900_000

reads:
  - ad_metrics(platform, level, from, to)      # our DB, hourly poller, never the UI
  - orders_panel(store, from, to)              # Shopify truth: orders, new-customer, returns
  - margin_table(sku)                          # COGS, freight, fees
  - stock_cover(sku)

  - holdout_readout(store, week)
  - experiment_budget(product)                 # how much this product has left to spend learning

  - raise_anomaly{kind, severity, note}

  bands: cpa_band_rules_v2          # deterministic daily rules
  allocator: thompson_v1            # deterministic weekly sampler

  daily_spend_cap_eur_per_store: 600
  portfolio_daily_cap_eur: 3_000
  max_change_pct_per_cell_per_day: 20
  cooldown_hours_per_cell: 48
  max_pauses_per_product_per_day: 3
  autonomy:
    auto_execute: [budget_change ≤ 20%, pause, negatives]
    maker_checker: [weekly channel reallocation, new campaign launch]
    human_signal: [budget above portfolio cap, any new platform, any creative to a cold audience with a claim]
  freeze_on: [signal_drift > 25%, payment_failure, stock_cover < 14d, anomaly severity ≥ high]

verify:
  - sum_of_proposed_budgets ≤ portfolio_daily_cap
  - every_cell_change_cites_exposure_and_cpa_evidence
  - no_change_to_a_cell_in_cooldown
  - no_acquisition_spend_on_sku_with_cover_lt_14d
  - holdout_geo_untouched

Two details matter more than the rest. First, the agent reads our copy of ad metrics, pulled hourly into Postgres, never the platform UI — so every decision can be replayed against the exact data that produced it. Second, propose_* is the only way anything reaches a platform; the gateway holds the API credentials, applies the caps, writes the ledger entry, and calls Meta or Google with an idempotency key. The model cannot spend money directly, by construction.

What a human sees each Monday

One page per product: spend, orders, CM2, the holdout-adjusted incremental figure, every change the agent made with the rule that caused it, the cells still below minimum exposure, and the remaining experiment budget. The merchandiser's job is to disagree with it — and because each line carries its rule and its evidence ids, disagreeing takes a minute rather than an afternoon.
